Recurrent Structural Policy Gradient for Partially Observable Mean Field Games

Clarisse Wibault
May 2026
Paper (arXiv) | MFAX Codebase | Interactive Colab Tutorial

In this Blog Post, I will introduce Recurrent Structural Policy Gradient (RSPG), a Hybrid Structural Method (defined below) that learns history-dependent policies for Partially Observable Mean Field Games with Public Information.

RSPG leverages two assumptions that are natural in many of the situations that we want to model.

  1. Individual State-Space is Low Dimensional: the individual state-space, over which the mean-field is defined, is low dimensional.
  2. Public Information: As well as knowing their individual state, agents receive shared observations of the aggregate state.

The first assumption is necessary to apply Hybrid Structural Methods in general, not just Recurrent Structural Policy Gradient. For example, the mean-field might be two-dimensional, over wealth and income in macroeconomics, or energy and interference in communication systems, or one-dimensional, over infection status in epidemiology. Regarding the second assumption, the public information might be stock prices, interest rates, reported infection levels or communication signals.

The main idea is that, because individual states are known, and observations of the aggregate state are shared, memory can be restricted to the history of shared aggregate observations. The intuition is that since agents directly observe their individual states, they only need to maintain a belief in the aggregate state. The main benefit is that we only have one history, rather than a number of possible histories that grows exponentially with time.

This way, RSPG maintains the same rate of convergence as memoryless Hybrid Structural Methods, while still allowing more realistic, history-dependent behaviour to be learned.

Mean Field Games with Common Noise

Mean-Field Games (MFGs) provide a principled framework for modelling strategic interactions in large population systems, such as financial markets, epidemiology, and autonomous swarms. Rather than tracking an intractable network of individual pairs, MFGs simplify the setting by considering a continuum of infinitesimal agents, where each player interacts exclusively with the empirical state distribution of the population—the mean field.

However, real-world systems are rarely isolated. They are frequently exposed to macro-level environmental shocks that affect all individuals simultaneously, mathematically defined as Common Noise. Common noise induces systemic environmental transitions that dynamically shift the mean field over time. This creates a co-evolutionary loop: the aggregate distribution determines macroeconomic conditions and individual rewards, while individual policies simultaneously advance tomorrow's population state distribution.

Dynamic Programming vs. RL vs. Hybrid Structural Methods

Algorithmic progress in MFGs has historically been limited due to a fundamental variance-tractability trade-off. Existing approaches generally separate into three paradigms:

  • Dynamic Programming (Exact Methods): While exact, these methods suffer from the curse of dimensionality. They scale poorly as the individual state space or history horizons grow, quickly becoming computationally intractable.
  • Model-Free Reinforcement Learning: These scale effectively to large spaces using deep neural networks, but they suffer from exceptionally high variance. They require millions of environment steps to converge because they treat the entire population distribution as part of a model-free, high-variance state representation.
  • Hybrid Structural Methods (HSMs): HSMs reduce variance while maintaining tractability by leveraging low-dimensional individual state and action spaces alongside known transition dynamics. They compute the exact expected return conditioned on Monte Carlo rollouts of common noise.

The structural core of an HSM is determined by how it updates population states:

Sample-Based vs. Analytic Mean-Field Updates: Instead of approximating the population distribution by simulating thousands of individual agent rollouts (Sample-Based), an Analytic Update uses a white-box "Pushforward" wrapper. It propagates the entire population distribution matrix analytically through the transition dynamics, providing exact, low-variance policy evaluation gradients.

Figure 2: Contrasting high-variance sample-based agent rollouts with the low-variance exact analytical pushforward of state distributions.

Partially Observable Mean Field Games with Common Noise

Despite achieving an order-of-magnitude faster convergence and superior sample efficiency, HSMs have been strictly restricted to fully observable settings. This assumes that individual agents can perfectly observe the true global population distribution. In realistic multi-agent settings like macroeconomics or communication networks, this assumption fails; agents only operate under partial observability.

Introducing partial observability into HSMs usually causes immediate mathematical intractability. Because every unique sequence of private observations yields a distinct individual history, the space of possible individual belief trajectories explodes exponentially over time. This history explosion shatters the unified population state mesh required to compute exact, analytical expected returns.

Recurrent Structural Policy Gradient

Our proposed framework, Recurrent Structural Policy Gradient (RSPG), is the first history-aware HSM designed to break this intractability in settings with public information.

The Fundamental Idea: By exploiting the presence of shared public observations, we can safely restrict the long-term memory of agents to the history of shared public observations. Because every player conditions their memory on the exact same stream of public metadata, the infinite tree of individual histories collapses back into a single, shared historical trajectory. This allows agents to maintain a shared recurrent belief distribution over hidden macroeconomic states, preserving the tractability of the analytic mean-field update.

The Practical Implementation: To facilitate future research into these settings, we introduce MFAX, our open-source, high-performance JAX-based framework for MFGs. MFAX leverages functional programming primitives like jax.vmap to map across discrete individual state meshes via piecewise-linear interpolation, and uses jax.lax.scan to efficiently optimize history-dependent recurrent neural network policies over long horizons.

By restructuring policy gradients around shared public histories, RSPG maintains excellent sample efficiency while allowing agents to successfully learn realistic, history-dependent behaviors in complex partially observable economies.
